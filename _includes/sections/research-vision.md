I lead the **[Model-Guided Uncertainty (MOGU) Lab](https://mogu-lab.github.io/)**, where we develop ML/AI methods to better **understand, predict, and prevent suicide**.

**Challenges.** Mental health data is often **sparse, noisy, and messy**, so purely data-driven ML/AI struggles to build reliable models to (a) identify when people are at risk, and (b) explain how the dynamics of suicide evolve over time. The natural remedy---leaning on human expertise---is itself hard: much of what makes clinicians effective, and much of what each of us understands about our own experiences, is **tacit and difficult to put into words**, let alone to translate into a predictive model.

**Our Approach.** We develop probabilistic (and Bayesian) methods that **elicit this hard-to-verbalize expertise from clinicians and patients** and **embed it directly into a model's structure and assumptions**, rather than bolting it on after the fact.
